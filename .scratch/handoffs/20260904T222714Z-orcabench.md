## Decisions and constraints

Human preference is authoritative for the arena rating. The two maintainers vote independently on anonymous artifacts, then readers can inspect their reasons and disagreement. Automated correctness checks, acceptability, preference, cost, and elapsed time remain separately visible.

A contestant freezes the native harness/version, controller and worker models, effort settings, prompts, skills, tools, environment, and resource limits. Task snapshots and attempt identifiers belong to runs. A configuration change creates a new version. The benchmark describes these maintainers' tasks and preferences.

Start with OpenCode after a working route test. Consider Harbor when its adapters and container tasks save work. Preserve native orchestration. Controller, child, retry, failed, and canceled-call usage all count; unknown usage stays unknown. The OpenCode stdout and Harbor adapter gap is documented with pinned source links in the integrations document.

The proposed screen is two controllers, Astra high and Fable 5.1 high, each with solo, same-model, Luna, or GLM workers. Six prepared tasks give 48 runs. First prove execution and judging on two tasks. Add an adaptive mixed worker pool later. The screen selects finalists; use fresh tasks for confirmation.

Begin with direct comparisons and transparent descriptive percentages. Fit batch Bradley-Terry later if coverage supports it, with an explicit tie treatment and optional Elo-like scale. The rating is relative preference, not a success probability. Never infer general task certainty from repeated ballots on the same artifacts.
